# SLAI Web Services Business Model

**Document Status:** Strategic company planning  
**Implementation Status:** Future / post-ServicesOS-V1 go-to-market lane  
**Last Updated:** 2026-09-01  
**Owner:** Jamie Brown / Stellar Logic AI

## Core Thesis

SLAI Web should not be treated as a random freelance side business.

It can become a **services-funded SaaS strategy**:

```text
Website / branding work
↓
Near-term cash flow
↓
Managed-web recurring revenue
↓
Workflow discovery
↓
ServicesOS lead / demo
↓
ServicesOS recurring revenue
↓
SLAI funds more product development
```

The website is often an easier pain point for a small-business owner to recognize than operational workflow fragmentation.

SLAI can enter through the front door, learn the business, improve its public presence, and later show how ServicesOS improves the operating workflow behind it.

## Product / Service Separation

### SLAI Web Build

One-time professional website project.

Working reference price for a straightforward small-business build:

> **Approximately $2,000 one time**

This is a planning reference, not an unconditional quote. Larger branding, SEO, custom content, photography coordination, integrations, or custom functionality may increase the project price.

### SLAI Managed Website

Current pricing decision:

> **$100/month**

Intended scope:

- hosting,
- SSL,
- monitoring,
- backup/recovery,
- technical maintenance,
- deployments,
- DNS/domain assistance,
- analytics/search-health monitoring,
- bug fixes,
- ServicesOS-to-website integration health where applicable.

Routine public business-data edits should increasingly be handled by the customer through ServicesOS rather than by manual support requests.

### ServicesOS

Current V1 pricing decision:

> **$100/month**

This replaces earlier $99.99-style pricing discussion.

ServicesOS should remain a separate product with a visible standalone value.

### Combined Customer

A customer using both:

```text
Managed Website  $100/month
ServicesOS       $100/month
---------------------------
Total            $200/month
```

The line items may appear on one invoice while remaining separate products/services.

## ServicesOS Pricing Rule

American Barbershop or another early design partner should not automatically receive a family/founder discount on ServicesOS.

Current intent:

> **Standard ServicesOS price remains $100/month unless Jamie explicitly creates a promotion.**

The value of real feedback does not require devaluing the SaaS subscription.

## Promotional Options

Promotions may be used tactically without permanently reducing list price.

Possible offers:

- first two months of Managed Website included after a paid website build,
- limited build discount tied to a defined managed-website commitment,
- free launch/DNS migration,
- free ServicesOS website connector setup,
- free Search Console / basic analytics setup,
- referral credits.

These are options, not permanent entitlements.

Preferred principle:

> Discount the lower-cost onboarding/service friction before discounting the long-term core SaaS value.

## Revenue Role

SLAI Web can create three revenue types:

1. **Build revenue** — upfront project cash.
2. **Managed-web revenue** — recurring service revenue.
3. **ServicesOS revenue** — recurring SaaS revenue.

Example fully converted customer at the working reference build price:

```text
Website build:        ~$2,000 one time
Managed website:      $100/month
ServicesOS:           $100/month
Recurring total:      $200/month
Annual recurring:     $2,400/year
```

## Founder Compensation Intent

Current founder planning intent:

- Jamie expects to need **at least $1,000 from each normal website build** as personal compensation while website builds are one of the only direct ways SLAI pays him.
- Recurring Managed Website and ServicesOS subscription revenue should primarily remain inside SLAI to fund company operations and growth.
- Remaining build proceeds, after direct project costs, tax reserves, and properly classified founder compensation, can also strengthen SLAI.

This is a **business-planning intent, not tax/accounting instructions**.

Before regular payments begin, SLAI should confirm the correct treatment with an accountant based on the company's legal/tax structure.

## Why Websites Can Lead to ServicesOS

During a website project SLAI naturally learns:

- how customers currently book,
- how schedules are managed,
- how staff receive work,
- how payments are collected,
- which tools are duplicated,
- what is still manual,
- how follow-up/rebooking works,
- how customer information is stored,
- where the owner loses time.

Those observations can reveal a legitimate ServicesOS fit.

Website customers may therefore become some of SLAI's warmest ServicesOS leads.

## Vertical Discovery

Web projects can also become a market-research channel.

If multiple businesses in one vertical repeatedly report the same operational pain, SLAI gains evidence for a future ServicesOS vertical.

Example:

```text
Barber websites
↓
Repeated Booksy / booking / rebooking pain
↓
Validated appointment-workflow gap
↓
ServicesOS Barber vertical earns deeper research
```

This is better than choosing verticals from theory alone.

## Productized-Service Guardrails

SLAI Web must not become a founder-time-heavy traditional agency.

Protect margins through:

- reusable website core,
- approved layout systems,
- limited revision rounds,
- clear scope,
- ServicesOS-driven business data,
- AI-assisted implementation,
- standard QA,
- standard deployment,
- no unlimited custom development inside the maintenance fee,
- separate quotes for major redesigns/features,
- protected ServicesOS engineering time.

## Delivery Economics

Current experiments show that Jamie + ChatGPT + Claude/Codex can already produce a strong small-business website concept in roughly two hours.

With:

- reusable core,
- 5–10 layout systems,
- ServicesOS tenant data,
- stable connector components,
- automated checks,

straightforward builds may eventually require around one hour or less of hands-on production time.

That efficiency should improve margin. It should **not automatically reduce the value-based client price**.

## Scale Illustration

Illustrative only:

If SLAI eventually delivered roughly one $2,000 website per business day across a 20-workday month:

```text
Build revenue:          ~$40,000/month
Founder $1,000/build:   ~$20,000 before tax treatment
Remaining gross build:  ~$20,000 before costs/taxes
```

If every one of those 20 clients also took both recurring services, that cohort could add:

```text
20 x $100 Managed Web = $2,000 new MRR
20 x $100 ServicesOS  = $2,000 new MRR
Total                 = $4,000 new MRR
```

This is not a forecast. It demonstrates why even much lower volume can become meaningful.

## Hiring Trigger

Do not hire simply because theoretical throughput is high.

A web hire becomes justified when real sales/QA/support load consistently interferes with founder focus.

Likely first role:

> **Web Implementation / QA Developer**

The role manages the production system rather than hand-building every site.

## Business Formalization Milestone

A first paying web customer can help fund formal company setup.

Planning checklist:

- secure appropriate SLAI domain(s),
- confirm company legal structure/registration,
- obtain required tax identification such as an EIN where applicable,
- confirm local/state licensing requirements,
- open dedicated business banking,
- establish bookkeeping,
- use client contracts/invoices,
- document owner contributions/reimbursements,
- confirm correct founder-payment treatment with an accountant.

Do not treat this checklist as legal or tax advice; verify requirements before acting.
